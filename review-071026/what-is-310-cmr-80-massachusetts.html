What Is 310 CMR 80? A Guide to Massachusetts Underground Storage Tank Regulations

A proposed resource for Massachusetts underground storage tank owners and operators.

If your business owns or operates an underground storage tank (UST) in Massachusetts, you've probably come across the term 310 CMR 80. Whether you're responsible for a gas station, municipal fueling facility, commercial fleet, or industrial property, understanding these regulations is one of the most important steps toward protecting your facility and staying compliant.

The good news is that 310 CMR 80 isn't meant to make operating a UST system more difficult. Its purpose is to establish standards that help prevent leaks, protect groundwater, and ensure underground storage tanks are operated safely and responsibly.

In this guide, we'll explain what 310 CMR 80 is, who it applies to, and the key requirements every UST owner or operator should understand.

What Is 310 CMR 80?

310 CMR 80 is the Massachusetts regulation that governs underground storage tank systems. Administered by the Massachusetts Department of Environmental Protection (MassDEP), the regulation establishes requirements for the installation, operation, inspection, maintenance, repair, and closure of UST systems.

The regulation became effective in 2015 and was updated in 2021 to align many state requirements with modern federal UST standards while continuing to address Massachusetts-specific environmental priorities.

The overall goal is simple: reduce the risk of releases that could contaminate soil, groundwater, and nearby communities.

Who Does 310 CMR 80 Apply To?

In general, 310 CMR 80 applies to owners and operators of underground storage tank systems that store more than 110 gallons of hazardous material, including petroleum products and other regulated substances.

Examples of facilities that commonly fall under these regulations include:

  • Retail fueling stations
  • Municipal fueling facilities
  • Commercial fleet operators
  • Industrial facilities
  • Transportation and logistics companies

Because every facility is different, it's important to understand how the regulations apply to your specific tank system.

What Does the Regulation Cover?

While the exact requirements vary depending on the system, 310 CMR 80 focuses on several core areas designed to improve safety and environmental protection.

These include:

  • Leak detection systems
  • Spill and overfill prevention
  • Routine inspections and testing
  • Operator training
  • Equipment maintenance
  • Recordkeeping and documentation

These requirements work together to identify potential problems early—before they become expensive repairs or environmental incidents.

Why Understanding the Regulation Matters

Many facility owners assume compliance begins when an inspector arrives. In reality, compliance is an ongoing process.

Understanding what 310 CMR 80 requires allows businesses to plan inspections, maintain accurate records, and address equipment issues before they lead to violations or costly downtime.

Taking a proactive approach is often far less expensive than reacting to an unexpected compliance issue.

Key Takeaways

  • 310 CMR 80 is the primary Massachusetts regulation governing underground storage tanks.
  • It generally applies to UST systems storing more than 110 gallons of hazardous material.
  • The regulation covers inspections, testing, leak detection, maintenance, operator training, and documentation.
  • Understanding the requirements helps facility owners stay compliant and reduce operational risk.
