What Are 250-RICR-140-25-1? A Guide to Rhode Island Underground Storage Tank Regulations

A practical overview of the Rhode Island rules that shape how covered UST systems are registered, monitored, tested, maintained, documented, and closed.

If your organization owns or operates an underground storage tank (UST) in Rhode Island, the rules that govern that system affect much more than an occasional inspection. They shape how the tank is registered, monitored, tested, maintained, documented, and eventually closed.

For gas stations, municipal fueling facilities, commercial fleets, and other sites that rely on stored fuel, understanding the state framework is an important first step toward safer operations and fewer compliance surprises.

This guide explains the role of Rhode Island Department of Environmental Management (RIDEM), what 250-RICR-140-25-1 covers, and the core responsibilities UST owners and operators should keep on their radar.

What Is 250-RICR-140-25-1?

Rhode Island's Rules and Regulations for Underground Storage Facilities Used for Regulated Substances and Hazardous Materials are administered by Rhode Island Department of Environmental Management (RIDEM).

The program covers UST registration, installation and modification, operation, testing, operator certification, release response, and closure. Rhode Island also requires certified Class A and Class B operators, and its Class B inspection checklist is used at least every 30 days.

The purpose is practical: reduce the chance of releases, identify equipment problems early, protect soil and groundwater, and create a clear record that a UST system is being operated responsibly.

Who Do the Rhode Island UST Rules Apply To?

The rules generally apply to owners and operators of covered UST systems that store petroleum or other regulated substances. The exact answer depends on the substance, tank use, capacity, location, and any state or federal exclusion.

Facilities commonly affected include:

  • Retail fueling stations
  • Municipal fueling facilities
  • Commercial fleet yards
  • Industrial and institutional properties
  • Emergency and critical-fueling locations

Because exemptions and special categories can be technical, owners should confirm their system's status instead of relying only on a general rule of thumb.

What Do the Regulations Cover?

Although requirements vary by system, the compliance framework commonly addresses:

  • Registration, notification, and permitting
  • Release detection and monitoring
  • Spill and overfill prevention
  • Testing, inspection, and maintenance
  • Operator training and emergency response
  • Financial responsibility and recordkeeping
  • Repair, upgrade, and closure procedures

These responsibilities work together. A passing test is important, but so are the inspection records, operator credentials, registrations, and follow-up actions that show the system remains compliant between tests.

Why Understanding the Rules Matters

UST compliance is ongoing. Knowing what RIDEM expects allows an owner or operator to schedule work before due dates, keep required records accessible, and respond promptly when an alarm, inspection, or test reveals a problem.

A proactive program is usually less disruptive and less expensive than correcting several overdue or undocumented items at once.

Key Takeaways

  • 250-RICR-140-25-1 is the central state framework for covered UST systems in Rhode Island.
  • RIDEM oversees the state's UST program.
  • Requirements may include registration, testing, monitoring, operator training, maintenance, release response, and documentation.
  • The specific requirements depend on the system, so site-level review matters.
